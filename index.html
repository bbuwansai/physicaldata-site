01  Why episodes

Clips capture actions.
Episodes capture consequences.

Acting is solved. Planning is close. Keeping track across hours of real work is not, and a clip cannot teach it: a clip is labeled for what happens inside it, and carries nothing about what it owes to the past. That gap is the data we make.

A clip · 30 seconds

Two pixels wide at the scale of the job. Label: "places cup on shelf"

An episode · 3 h 14 min

Every placement linked to its retrieval. Label: "glasses back to the cupboard · parked 00:49:40"

02  What we record

Not hours of footage. Events that depend on each other.

Delayed consequences

The tray soaks, the oven heats, the boxes wait by the door. Both ends labeled and linked, fifteen minutes to two hours apart.

Interruptions

The phone rings, the trash overflows. We record the suspended goal, the resume point and the return.

Temporary placements

The cupboard is full, so the glasses wait on a shelf. The placement opens an obligation; the retrieval two hours later closes it.

Mistakes and recovery

The states clean demonstrations never reach, and the paths back from them. Nothing is re-shot.

Repeats

The same job in the same place, twenty or more times, with the starting state changed on purpose. A distribution of decisions, not one lucky run.

03  The test

Don't take our word for it. We run the experiment on your free episode.

A

Short clips

The same footage cut into 30 to 60 second clips, shuffled.

B

Clips with a summary

The same clips, each with a written summary of what came before.

C

The full episode

Full continuity plus our decision, obligation and state annotations.

Ask a model, at moments the current frame cannot answer: what is unfinished, where is the tray, what should I resume. If C wins, and wins by more as the gap widens, this data does something clips cannot. If it doesn't, you have lost nothing.
